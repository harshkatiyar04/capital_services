The Full Picture

The Challenge

When Storm Eunice Took the Roof

Named storm winds tore tiles from the rear pitch of this 1930s High Wycombe cottage, opened the roof covering, and drove rain into the loft and first-floor bedrooms. Ceilings sagged, plaster failed, and water tracked into the landing and bathroom.

The owners needed the building made weathertight overnight, a clear structural view for LV=, and a reinstatement programme that would not leave the cottage open to further weather for weeks.

Our Approach

Seal the Envelope. Then Rebuild.

Emergency tarping secured the roof the same day. A structural survey confirmed the extent of rafter and ceiling damage, and scaffolding was programmed immediately so permanent roof works could follow without delay.

Internal drying, plaster repairs, and decoration ran in parallel once the envelope was closed. Mark Thompson coordinated six trades across a ten-week programme, returning Mr & Mrs Whitfield to a dry, fully reinstated home with LV= documentation complete.

Project Timeline

Every phase managed, documented, and delivered on schedule.

Phase 01
Emergency Tarping
Roof made weathertight the same day with professional tarping to stop further water ingress into the cottage.
Phase 02
Structural Survey
Full inspection of rafters, roof covering, and water-affected ceilings, with a clear schedule agreed with LV=.
Phase 03
Scaffolding & Roof Works
Scaffold erected, damaged covering stripped, and permanent roof reinstatement completed to match the existing pitch.
Phase 04
Internal Drying
Targeted drying of loft, bedrooms, and landing with moisture readings logged until surfaces were ready for finish works.
Phase 05
Plaster & Decoration
Failed ceilings and plaster made good, then full decoration to restore rooms to pre-storm condition.
Phase 06
Sign-Off
Final inspection, snagging, and formal sign-off with the homeowners and LV= documentation complete.
